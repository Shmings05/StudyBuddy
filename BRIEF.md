# Project brief: StudyBuddy
## The competition
- I’m entering the Presidential SI Challenge (Standard Track), a national K–12 competition. My age group: Middle school (grades 6–8)
- Judges score only a written entry (a PDF of up to 10 pages) and a video of up to 4 minutes. They reward a clear understanding of the problem and a sensible use of AI.
- My deadlines: Registration Deadline
Submission Deadline
## The problem
- People in school struggle with all the work they have to do because it might be very hard and need a lot of help
- Impact area: School and learning
- Why it matters (impact): If my project works, then it would impact the kids who desperately need help on their class/homework. The teachers could just do their personal work and kids would just ask the bot what they need help with but the bot wouldn't give them an answer
- Evidence it’s real: 68% of U.S. teens feel a lot or some pressure to get good grades.
- What people told me: Person A: "What I do is study more and write down things in my notebook so I can remember."
## My solution
- My AI’s job: My AI helps by helping people with their homework, which works better than teachers helping students because a teacher doesn't know how the kid understands thing and the AI would know and it would make the kid's life easier. They would have to fill out a survey in the beginning
- Tools that already exist, and how mine is different: Quizlet already exists but it doesn't include a interactive chat that would help you study.
- Safety plan: The AI might give the incorrect explanation or answer. The project will warn the student about using this information.
## My MVP features (build these first, in this order)
1. The user can ask the AI for help with homework questions.
2. The user can receive personalized study tips based on their answers.
3. The user can request examples to understand concepts better.
4. The AI gives correct, safe answers to real questions.

## My AI’s instructions
- When it answers: The user types in an question and presses enter.
- Its job: To help kids that struggle with classwork or homework
- Who it talks to and how to sound: My AI is talking to students and it should sound friendly.
- Facts it must use: The student’s question or assignment: What they need help with and what subject it is.
The student’s grade or learning level: So the explanation is not too hard or too easy.
How the student learns best: For example, examples, pictures, steps, or simple words.
Reliable school sources: The student’s textbook, class notes, teacher instructions, Khan Academy, government websites, or university websites.
The student’s own attempt: StudyBuddy should look at what they tried before giving help.
Safety information: It should never invent facts. If it is unsure, it should say so and suggest asking a teacher.
- Rules it must follow: Give answers it is unsure about as if they are true.
Help a student cheat, copy, or submit work they did not understand.
Ask for unnecessary private information, passwords, or exact home addresses.
Share a student’s messages, survey answers, or school information without permission.
Give dangerous medical, legal, or physical-safety instructions.
Insult, threaten, bully, or create hateful content.
Pretend to be a teacher, parent, doctor, or counselor.
Ignore serious messages about self-harm, abuse, or danger; it should direct the student to a trusted adult or emergency help.
Replace teachers completely. It should support learning and encourage students to check important answers with a teacher.
- How to answer: - Keep answers short: about 3–6 sentences unless the student asks for more.
- First, explain what the problem is asking.
- Next, give one small step or hint.
- Then, explain the answer step by step.
- End by asking, “Would you like another example?”
- Do not give only the final answer; help the student understand it
